THE HEART OF THE HOUSE

The croissants
come from our ovens

“Everything will be baked in-house,” the owners say — and the croissant is the proof item. Laminated dough, real butter, baked on-site before opening, out of the case from 6:30 a.m.

A Sacramento chef, trained in France

The pastry program is led by Antonio Coriano — born in Sacramento, trained in France, and back home running the case at JEYAS. Before the café he spent time as pastry chef de partie at Protégé, the Michelin-starred restaurant in Palo Alto, most recently through June 2026.

His Sactown résumé is part of the menu’s DNA: executive pastry chef at Old Soul Co., and lead macaron baker at Ginger Elizabeth Chocolates. That’s a decade and a half of pastry kitchens pointed at one thing: what comes out of the oven at JEYAS.

The inspiration for the café’s French line comes from Coriano’s training — specialty croissants first, then the wider pastry program and the dessert list that keeps the kitchen alive until midnight.

Why it matters

A croissant is the hardest thing a bakery does on purpose: days of lamination, butter at the exact right temperature, an oven that knows the dough. Shops that buy in par-baked goods still serve “fresh croissants.” JEYAS’s owners built the announcement around one sentence — everything will be baked in-house.

The lineup, the chef’s background and the plans on this page come from the opening announcement and press coverage. Items beyond the croissant will be published as the menu is finalized.
